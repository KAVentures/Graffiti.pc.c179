## Mathematical audit

The selected components have size at least two. Assign every unselected vertex to a selected neighbor, forming connected blocks. In a tree an unselected vertex has exactly one neighbor in its own block: two attachments or an edge to another unselected vertex in the same block would create a cycle. Thus the quotient has exactly c-1 edges between blocks. Let B be the non-degree-two vertices adjacent to degree-two vertices. For the selected members of B and the unselected members with a selected degree-two neighbor, the latter can be injected into selected degree-two vertices (each has a selected neighbor, so at most one unselected neighbor). The two recipient sets are disjoint. The remaining z members of B cannot be leaves, because their sole neighbor, a degree-two vertex, would be forced into D. Hence each has at least two interblock incidences, and each has an unselected degree-two neighbor, giving another incidence at that degree-two endpoint. These additional incidences are distinct and not counted at B endpoints. Therefore 3z <= 2(c-1), so 3b <= 3k+2c-2 <= 4k-2. This applies to every total dominating set; minimization proves the result. Edge cases D2 empty and a tree with one selected component are covered by the same count.
