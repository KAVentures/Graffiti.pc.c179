## Mathematical review

All supports are forced. For n>=3, a leaf is not a support vertex, and attaching leaves to the induced support graph does not change component count. Choose one representative per support component; representatives are independent. If D is total dominating and A is D minus representatives, every representative has an A-neighbor. Every vertex therefore has distance at most two to A. Along a diameter path (a geodesic), two vertices in a radius-two ball have distance at most four, so at most five path vertices lie in that ball. This yields diam(T)+1 <= 5|A|. Since rad(T)=ceil(diam(T)/2), one has rad(T)<=floor(5|A|/2) and rad(T)+1<=3|A| for integer |A|>=1 (check a=1 separately or use ceil(a/2)>=1). This proves the archived inequality and both strengthenings.

Sharp family: take the path on vertices 0 through 5m-1 and add a leaf at each index 1 or 3 modulo 5. All 2m supports are isolated from one another; the original path is a diameter path because added leaves occur only at interior vertices, and the distance between two new leaves is at most 5m-1. The selected positions 1,2,3 in each block dominate every vertex, including one another, and have size 3m. The lower bound forces equality. The radius is ceil((5m-1)/2); the asymptotic ratios (gamma_t-q)/(diam+1) and (gamma_t-q)/rad are 1/5 and tend to 2/5 respectively.
